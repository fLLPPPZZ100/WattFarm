## 1. Executive summary

The payout formula is the right *shape* and the concurrency work around it (row locks, `Decimal` money, per-player transaction isolation, the ledger) is genuinely solid. The economy sitting on top of it is not viable, for one structural reason and one blocking bug.

**The blocking bug:** no player receives mining income at all. `runPayoutCycle` only pays users who have a `MiningAllocation` row, nothing creates that row on signup, and the only UI that could create it is deliberately unmounted. The entire mining loop is dark for any account created through the current client.

**The structural reason:** the economy pairs an **unbounded cost curve** with a **bounded income curve**. Panel *n* costs `10 × 1.15^n`, which grows forever. Income is `rate/(rate + 40) × 50`, which can never exceed 7,200 VLT/day no matter what is built. Those two curves cross early and then diverge violently:

| Purchase | Cost | Income added | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Panel #2 | 12 VLT | +205 VLT/day | **1.3 hours** |
| Panel #21 | 164 VLT | +84 VLT/day | 2 days |
| Panel #41 | 2,679 VLT | +44 VLT/day | 61 days |
| Panel #56 | 21,796 VLT | +30 VLT/day | **724 days** |

That is a **12,900x drift in return on investment across a single grid**. It is not a difficulty curve, it is a wall. The same arithmetic makes the endgame sink actively hostile: unlocking and filling grid row 5 costs 1.07M VLT and returns +359 VLT/day — an **8-year payback**. Row 8 pays back in 5,770 years. No rational player buys either, so the only endgame sink is never used and VLT accumulates without limit.

Meanwhile emission has no global cap. The "50 VLT budget" is a per-player asymptote, not a pool: 1,000 full-grid players mint 4.58M VLT/day, 636x the nominal budget. That is survivable only while VLT is a private score, and it permanently blocks a marketplace, trading, or any shared-value feature.

**The fix is a change of method, not of constants.** Today ROI is an accident that falls out of two independently-chosen curves. It should be a design *input*: pick the payback window you want players to feel, then derive prices from the marginal yield the payout formula actually produces. Section 6 sets this out; Section 7 shows a calibrated five-season progression where payback stays inside a 4–20 day band instead of drifting by four orders of magnitude.

## 2. The economy as built

### 2.1 Faucets

| Source | Rate | Cap | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mining payout | `rate/(rate+40) × 50` per 10 min | 7,200 VLT/day/player (asymptotic) | **Requires a `MiningAllocation` row that nothing creates** |
| Minigames | EV 0.55 VLT/play | ~25 VLT/day at 45 plays | 96.95% of plays pay nothing |
| Starting grant | 50 VLT once | — | `auth.js` |

### 2.2 Sinks

| Sink | Cost | Recurring? | Used in practice? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solar panels | `10 × 1.15^owned` | No | Yes, until the ROI wall |
| Single mount | 15 VLT flat | No | Only the first one |
| Double mount | 45 VLT flat | No | Yes |
| Grid expansion | `50,000 × 4^(rows-4)` | No | **No — 8 to 5,770 year payback** |
| Avatars | 50 / 100 / 200 VLT | No | **No — none ship art in the client** |

There is **no recurring sink of any kind**: no upkeep, no maintenance, no decay, no consumables, no fees, no burn. Every sink is a one-time purchase. An economy whose sinks are all one-shot has a guaranteed terminal state where income continues and spending stops.

### 2.3 Measured progression

Cheapest path to a full 56-cell grid, starting from the 50 VLT grant:

| Panel | Day | Unit price | W/s | Income |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.00 | 10 | 1.00 | 176/day |
| 10 | 0.41 | 35 | 12.25 | 1,688/day |
| 20 | 0.87 | 142 | 24.75 | 2,752/day |
| 30 | 2.00 | 576 | 37.25 | 3,472/day |
| 40 | 5.62 | 2,329 | 49.75 | 3,991/day |
| 56 | **39.78** | 21,796 | 69.75 | 4,576/day |

39.8 days and 168,268 VLT to fill the grid. The pacing shape is correct (purchases decelerate: 1.6h between early panels, 4.8 days between the last two). The problem is what happens after day 40 — and that the last third of the grid is a strictly irrational purchase that players will make anyway because it is the only thing to buy.

## 3. Findings

### F1 — BLOCKER: nobody is paid

`services/miningPayout.js`:

```js
const allocations = await prisma.miningAllocation.findMany({ where: { network } });
if (allocations.length === 0) continue;
```

Payouts are driven entirely off `MiningAllocation` rows. Those rows are created in exactly one place — `POST /api/mining/allocations` — and its only client is `MiningAllocationPanel.jsx`, whose own header says it is "intentionally not rendered anywhere". Nothing in `auth.js`, the seed, or a migration creates a default row.

Consequence: a player signs up, receives 50 VLT, buys a mount and a panel, places it, sees "Active Power: 1.00 W/s" — and earns nothing, forever, with no error anywhere.

This likely hides in development because older accounts still have rows from when the panel was mounted, and because `scripts/run-payout.mjs` exercises the same code path against those accounts.

**Fix:** create a default `{ solar: 100 }` allocation in the same upsert that grants the starting VLT, and treat a missing allocation as 100% to the only live network rather than as zero. Add a backfill migration for existing users.

### F2 — The allocation slider is a power incinerator

`effectiveRate = fullRate × (allocation.percentage / 100)`, but `NETWORK_SOURCES.wind` and `.hydro` are `null`, so those networks never pay. Any power allocated to them is silently destroyed. A 34/33/33 split earns 12.69 VLT/cycle against 25.00 for 100% solar — the control has one correct setting and every other position is a loss. Two thirds of the nominal emission budget (100 of 150 VLT/cycle) is orphaned.

Parking the UI was the right call. The remaining bug is that the *server* still multiplies by a percentage that can only hurt.

**Fix:** until wind and hydro have placeable assets, normalise allocations across live networks only (so any split behaves as 100% solar). Keep the sliders hidden.

### F3 — CRITICAL: unbounded cost against bounded income

Covered in Section 1. Two further consequences worth recording:

- Panel #90 costs 2.90M VLT — more than a **full year** of income at the theoretical ceiling.
- Panel #247 costs 9.83e15 VLT, past `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`. `Decimal` protects the database, but any client-side arithmetic on that value silently loses precision.

### F4 — CRITICAL: grid expansion destroys value

| Row | Unlock | Panels to fill | Total | Income gain | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 50,000 | 1.02e6 | 1.07e6 | +359/day (+7.8%) | **8 years** |
| 6 | 200,000 | 7.18e6 | 7.38e6 | +273/day (+5.5%) | 74 years |
| 7 | 800,000 | 5.08e7 | 5.16e7 | +214/day (+4.1%) | 661 years |
| 8 | 3,200,000 | 3.60e8 | 3.63e8 | +172/day (+3.2%) | 5,770 years |

The unlock price itself is a rounding error; the panels needed to make the row useful are what kill it. Going from 4 rows to 8 rows — a 2x space increase — raises income by only **22%**, because the share function is concave and a full grid already captures 63.6% of the budget.

This exposes a general law worth internalising: **under a concave share function, every capacity purchase has strictly worse payback than the one before it.** So capacity can never be priced on a geometric curve of its own. It has to be priced against the marginal yield at the moment of purchase, or not sold for currency at all.

### F5 — CRITICAL: emission is per-player and linear in population

| Players | Minted/day | vs nominal budget |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4,582 | 0.6x |
| 100 | 458,182 | 63.6x |
| 10,000 | 45,818,182 | 6,363.6x |

`NETWORK_POWER_BASELINE` is a constant, so each player runs a private difficulty contest against a fixed opponent. There is no shared pool, no competition, and no supply control. This is the single largest architectural gap versus the RollerCoin model it is based on.

### F6 — The starting grant is misaligned with the correct first purchase

The grant is 50 VLT. A double mount plus a panel costs 55. So the first purchase is forced to be a single mount, which is permanently the worse asset (1.00 W/s per panel against 1.25) and cannot be sold or refunded. The player's very first economic decision is a trap they cannot see or undo.

Grant 60 VLT, or price the first mount lower, or make the tutorial hand out the mount directly.

### F7 — Minigames are economically irrelevant

45 plays/day of active clicking yields 24.8 VLT — **0.54%** of a full-grid farm's idle income, 2.5% at five panels. The cooldown ladder (10s → 600s) throttles the mechanic further. Rational players stop playing immediately.

This is the most important *design* inversion versus RollerCoin, and it is discussed in Section 5.

### F8 — No recurring sink, therefore no equilibrium

See 2.2. A healthy virtual economy needs drains that scale with a player's wealth, so that a bigger farm costs more to run. WattFarm has none. Whatever the faucet is set to, balances trend to infinity.

### F9 — VLT is a closed loop with no terminal value

There is no withdrawal, no marketplace, no trade, no conversion. BTC/ETH/SOL/TRX have coin art and a currency switcher in `AppShell.jsx` but no columns, no routes, and no emission — they are decoration. Without an exit or a competitive use, accumulating VLT has no meaning, which removes the tension that makes an economy interesting.

### F10 — Client/server drift on prices and formulas

- `MyAssets.jsx` prices the double mount at **25 VLT**; the server charges **45**. The purchase will fail or surprise.
- `MyAssets.jsx` advertises a `starter-pack` (25 VLT, "1 Solar Panel + 50 VLT bonus") with no server endpoint.
- `GameStats.jsx` invents `blockReward = totalPower × 0.05` and `networkHash = totalPower × 1000`. Both are unrelated to the server. The HUD shows a player an estimated reward of 3.5 VLT while the server pays 31.8.

Prices and projections must come from `GET /api/assets/catalog` and the payout formula, never from client constants.

### F11 — Audit trail is incomplete

`LedgerEntry` covers purchases, avatar unlocks and grid expansion, but **not** mining payouts or minigame winnings — those live in separate tables with different shapes. There is no single append-only view of a balance's history, so `vltBalance` cannot be reconstructed or reconciled. The schema comment also lists only `"purchase" | "avatar-unlock"` while the code writes `'grid-expansion'`.

Make `LedgerEntry` the one source of truth for every balance change, credit and debit, with `balanceAfter` on all of them. That is what makes an economy auditable when someone inevitably finds an exploit.

## 4. Root cause

One sentence: **a bounded faucet is financing an unbounded sink, and the exchange rate between them was never a design parameter.**

```
income(r) = r/(r + 40) × 50 × 144     →  asymptote 7,200 VLT/day     (bounded, concave)
cost(n)   = 10 × 1.15^n               →  unbounded                   (convex, no ceiling)
```

Both curves are individually reasonable. Nobody ever computed `cost / Δincome`. That ratio — payback time — is the number players actually feel, and it is currently an emergent side effect that swings by 12,900x.

Everything in Section 6 follows from inverting the dependency: **choose payback, derive price.**

## 5. What to take from RollerCoin — and what not to

RollerCoin's model, for reference: mining power is bought with the soft currency (RLT) or earned by playing; a fixed block reward is issued roughly every 10 minutes and split between players **in the same league** in proportion to their power; payouts are in real tokens (BTC, ETH, DOGE) that can be withdrawn. ([RollerCoin FAQ — block reward](https://faq.rollercoin.com/rollercoin/f.a.q./basics/3.-what-is-a-block-reward), [RollerCoin FAQ — RLT](https://faq.rollercoin.com/rollercoin/f.a.q./basics/10.-what-is-rlt)) *Content was rephrased for compliance with licensing restrictions.*

### Take these four

1. **A genuinely shared, fixed pool.** `share = yours / (everyone's + floor) × pool`. Total emission is capped by construction. This is F5's fix and the precondition for every social or economic feature that comes later.
2. **Difficulty that comes from the network, not from your own inventory.** In RollerCoin your share falls because *others* build, not because *you* bought too much. WattFarm currently punishes the player for their own progress, which is why the ROI wall exists.
3. **Leagues.** A fixed pool split globally is brutal to latecomers. Bracketing players by power, each bracket with its own pool, is what makes a capped economy fair to someone joining in year two.
4. **Generational equipment.** New tiers with much better power-per-cell, at slightly worse power-per-VLT. Old equipment decays in *relative* value without a nerf patch. This replaces exponential quantity pricing entirely.

### Do not take these two

1. **Real-token payouts.** They convert a game-balance problem into a treasury, KYC, and regulatory problem. Keep a second currency (Section 6.2) as the store of value and decide later whether it ever bridges out. Design the economy so it works with a purely internal terminal currency.
2. **RollerCoin's game feel.** Their power comes from a flat inventory of miners in rooms; the games are a faucet bolted on the side. WattFarm has a **spatial grid where placement is server-authoritative** — that is the differentiator, and Section 6.7 argues it should be the primary strategic axis rather than a skin over the same idle loop.

### And invert this one

RollerCoin's central loop is **play the games to get power**. WattFarm's minigames are a lottery paying 0.54% of idle income. The active loop should be where the interesting decisions are, not a vestigial slot machine (F7).

## 6. Proposed economy

### 6.1 Principle: ROI is an input

Define one constant the whole economy hangs from:

```
TARGET_PAYBACK_DAYS = 14
```

Then derive every price from the yield the payout formula actually produces at that moment:

```
marginalYield  = poolPerDay / (totalNetworkWatts + npcFloor)     // VLT per day per watt
price(ΔW, t)   = ΔW × marginalYield × TARGET_PAYBACK_DAYS × TIER_TAX^t
```

Prices become a *function of network state* rather than hardcoded constants. This is what keeps ROI in band automatically as the population and the frontier tier move.

Practical guardrails, because volatile prices are hostile:
- Recompute on a **published schedule** (weekly, or at season boundaries), never per request.
- Never raise a price inside an active session; show the next price and when it takes effect.
- Persist every recomputation in the ledger so a player can see why a price moved.

### 6.2 Two currencies

| | Role | Faucet | Sink |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VLT** (soft) | Buys equipment, pays upkeep | Minigames, quests, events, season rewards | Purchases, upkeep, consumables, crafting |
| **kWh** (hard) | Store of value, prestige, marketplace unit | Mining pool only, hard-capped per cycle | Marketplace fees (burned), season entry, top-tier equipment |

Two currencies decouple the two problems. VLT can be generous, frequent, and tuned for engagement without inflating anything that matters. kWh is scarce, capped, and is what a leaderboard or a marketplace is denominated in. It also gives minigames a real job: they mint VLT, which buys the equipment that mines kWh — RollerCoin's structure, with the active loop restored to the centre.

### 6.3 Shared pool with endogenous difficulty

```
poolPerCycle   = K × max(1, activeMiners)^0.85
npcFloor       = 0.6 × cells(4 rows) × wattsOf(frontierTier)
share_i        = r_i / (Σ r_j + npcFloor) × poolPerCycle
```

- The `Σ r_j` term makes difficulty endogenous: your share falls when *others* build (F5 fixed, RollerCoin lesson 2).
- Total emission is capped at `poolPerCycle` by construction — no global cap needed as a separate mechanism.
- `npcFloor` keeps the curve meaningful when the game is small or empty, and anchoring it to the frontier tier is what produces automatic obsolescence.
- The `^0.85` exponent is the important nuance. A strictly fixed pool means per-player income falls as `1/N`, which punishes growth. Sublinear growth makes it fall as `N^-0.15` instead:

| Players | Current total | Proposed total | Current/player | Proposed/player |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4,582 | 2,817 | 4,582 | 2,817 |
| 100 | 458,182 | 141,184 | 4,582 | 1,412 |
| 1,000 | 4,581,818 | 999,509 | 4,582 | 1,000 |
| 10,000 | 45,818,182 | 7,075,984 | 4,582 | 708 |

Doubling the population costs each player ~10% of income. A ramp, not a cliff — and aggregate emission is now sublinear instead of linear.

### 6.4 Tiers replace quantity scaling

Delete the `1.15^owned` multiplier. Panels of a given tier have a **flat price**; progression comes from tiers, and space stays the binding constraint.

| Tier | W/cell | Power step |
|---|---|---|
| Mk1 | 1 | — |
| Mk2 | 3 | 3x |
| Mk3 | 9 | 3x |
| Mk4 | 27 | 3x |
| Mk5 | 81 | 3x |

`TIER_TAX = 1.15` makes each tier ~15% worse per watt than the last, so upgrading is always correct but never free, and payback drifts gently upward instead of exploding.

### 6.5 Recurring sinks (this is what makes it sustainable)

The single biggest gap (F8). Recommended, in order of value:

1. **Upkeep / efficiency decay.** Panels drift from 100% to ~60% efficiency over roughly a week of uptime. Servicing costs VLT proportional to installed value — **or is free if the player completes a minigame**. This one mechanic fixes three findings at once: it creates a wealth-proportional recurring drain (F8), it makes minigames economically central (F7), and it gives a reason to log in daily. Size it at **20% of gross income** (see the table in the simulator): felt, not punitive.
2. **Marketplace with a burn.** Player-to-player equipment trading in kWh with a 5% fee, burned. Only viable after 6.3 caps emission — a marketplace on top of today's linear minting would be immediately hyperinflationary.
3. **Crafting / fusion.** Three Mk *n* panels plus VLT produce one Mk *n+1*. Drains both currency and item supply, and gives obsolete equipment an exit that is not a refund.
4. **Consumables.** Timed boosts (inverter overclock: +20% for 4 hours). Pure sink, no permanent power creep, naturally repeat-purchased.
5. **Seasons.** Entry fee in VLT, rewards in kWh and cosmetics. Bounded, repeatable, and the natural home for leaderboards.

Grid rows should **stop being a VLT sink** (F4). Under a concave share function, space can never pay for itself. Make rows a progression reward — season completion, achievements, tier milestones — and put the currency sinks where they do not distort ROI.

### 6.6 Leagues and seasons

Bracket players by power (Bronze / Silver / Gold / Platinum), each with its own `poolPerCycle`. A season advances the frontier tier, which raises `npcFloor`, which devalues old equipment automatically. Latecomers start in Bronze against peers instead of against year-one whales — the mechanism that lets a capped-emission economy stay fair over time.

### 6.7 Space as the strategic axis — the actual differentiator

`PlacedMount` is already server-authoritative with `col`/`row` coordinates, and nothing currently reads those coordinates for anything but bounds checking. That is an entire unused design axis, and it is what makes WattFarm not a RollerCoin reskin. Placement should *matter*:

- **Adjacency.** An inverter grants +15% to the panels orthogonally adjacent to it. Now layout is a puzzle with an optimum, not a filling exercise.
- **Terrain.** Some cells get more sun (row-based or per-account seeded). Creates asymmetry between farms and a reason to look at someone else's.
- **Shading.** Tall equipment reduces output of the cell behind it. Introduces a real tradeoff for density.
- **Districts.** Contiguous same-tier blocks earn a set bonus. Rewards planning ahead over buying greedily.

These are the reason to have a grid at all, they cost nothing in emission, they add depth without adding faucets, and none of them require the payout formula to change.

## 7. Calibrated result

Prices derived from `TARGET_PAYBACK_DAYS = 14` at each season's difficulty. Full generation in `economy-sim.mjs --model=proposed`.

**Season 3 (frontier Mk3, difficulty 302 W/s):**

| Tier | W/cell | Price/panel | VLT/W | Full farm | VLT/day | % pool | Farm payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mk1 | 1 | 99 | 99.0 | 56 W | 1,125 | 15.6% | 4.9 days |
| Mk2 | 3 | 342 | 113.8 | 168 W | 2,571 | 35.7% | 7.4 days |
| Mk3 | 9 | 1,178 | 130.9 | 504 W | 4,500 | 62.5% | 14.7 days |

**Payback across all five seasons and all fifteen tier/season combinations stays between 4.3 and 19.4 days** — against 1.3 hours to 724 days today.

Two properties fall out for free:

**Automatic obsolescence.** A player who never upgrades past Mk1 sees their share decay on its own as the frontier advances:

| Frontier | Mk1 full farm earns | Share of pool |
|---|---|---|
| Mk1 | 4,500/day | 62.5% |
| Mk3 | 1,125/day | 15.6% |
| Mk5 | 145/day | 2.0% |

No nerf patch, no angry changelog — the same equipment simply matters less as the network grows past it.

**Catch-up pricing.** Because price tracks current marginal yield, old tiers get cheaper every season (Mk1: 878 → 296 → 99 → 33 → 11 VLT). A new player buys their on-ramp at a fraction of what the founders paid, which is exactly how RollerCoin keeps late entry viable.

One calibration note: at Season 1 the entry tier prices out at 878 VLT, far above any sensible starting grant. The **entry tier should be deliberately subsidised** below the ROI curve — sold at a loss, or handed out by the tutorial. The on-ramp is the one place where correct ROI is the wrong answer.

## 8. Invariants to hold

These are the things to assert in tests and watch on a dashboard. Each one, violated, is a bug that was previously invisible.

| Invariant | Bound | Catches |
|---|---|---|
| Marginal payback of any purchase | 3–30 days | F3, F4 — the ROI wall |
| Payback spread across one season | < 10x | Pacing collapse |
| Total emission per cycle | ≤ `poolPerCycle` | F5 — uncapped minting |
| Recurring sink as % of gross income | 15–25% | F8 — no equilibrium |
| Median player net balance, 30-day trend | Bounded, not monotonic | Runaway accumulation |
| Active (minigame) income as % of total | 15–40% | F7 — dead active loop |
| Client-displayed price vs `/catalog` | Exact match | F10 — drift |
| Σ ledger deltas per user | `== vltBalance` | F11 — unauditable balances |

**Phase 0 — stop the bleeding (hours)**
1. F1: default `MiningAllocation` on signup + backfill. Nobody is being paid.
2. F2: normalise allocation across live networks only.
3. F10: delete client price constants; read `/catalog`. Fix `GameStats` to use the real formula.
4. F6: grant 60 VLT, or hand out the first mount.

**Phase 1 — make ROI a parameter (days)**
5. F11: route every balance change through `LedgerEntry`.
6. Add `apps/api/scripts/economy-sim.mjs` to CI as an assertion on the Section 8 invariants.
7. F3: replace the `1.15^owned` multiplier with flat per-tier pricing derived from `TARGET_PAYBACK_DAYS`.
8. F4: stop selling grid rows for VLT; convert them to progression rewards.

**Phase 2 — make it an economy (weeks)**
9. F5: shared pool with endogenous difficulty (6.3). This is the gate for everything after it.
10. F8: upkeep with the minigame-as-labour discount (6.5.1) — the sustainability keystone.

13. Second currency (6.2) and leagues (6.6).
14. Spatial mechanics: adjacency, terrain, shading (6.7). The differentiator.
15. Marketplace with burn — only after 9 and 10 are live and stable.

## 10. Verdict

The engineering under this economy is better than the economy. Row locks, `Decimal` money, server-authoritative placement, per-player payout isolation, a real ledger — that is the hard part, and it is done properly.

What is missing is a single number that was never chosen: how long a purchase should take to pay for itself. Everything diagnosed above is downstream of that omission. Pick 14 days, derive prices from it, cap the pool, and add one recurring sink — and the same codebase becomes a sustainable economy with RollerCoin's ROI feel and a spatial game that RollerCoin does not have.

But fix F1 first. Right now the farm does not pay.
